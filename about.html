Who we are

In Palestine Today says it aims to show “the human side of events in Palestine”, and to bring “the world closer to Palestine, one story at a time”. It publishes daily updates, stories, visuals and reports in English.

Palestine time

The site keeps Palestine’s clock. Every time on it is Palestine time, the IANA zone Asia/Hebron, whatever the reader’s own clock says. The page also keeps Palestine’s light. It is pale at noon over Jerusalem, warm in the afternoon and violet at dusk, and it is dark at night there, even when it is morning where you read.

The homepage tells one day in order, from midnight to the last report before the next. Each part of the day is printed in the light it happened in, and every report sits on the sun’s real path in the Day Dial at the minute it was filed. The full stop in our name is the sun, at its real height above the horizon now. The identity book explains the system.

How we source

Each report names the source it was read from and links to it. Each figure names who counted it, and the date of the count. An accusation is attributed in the same sentence that makes it.

We read the official Palestinian news agency WAFA, the UN’s humanitarian office OCHA and UN News, prisoners’ institutions, the Colonization and Wall Resistance Commission, municipal and health authorities, and international outlets. When two sources disagree, both are reported, as on 14 October, when the agency’s count of the dead changed between evening and midnight.

Times are when the source published, in Palestine time, not when the event happened; a report says so when the two differ. Children are never named. Private people are named only with their consent.

Photographs from our own archive or from Wikimedia Commons carry the date and place they were taken. None is presented as showing an event it does not show, and none is tinted or graded.

Corrections

When we get something wrong, we correct the report, say what changed and when, and keep the change visible at the end of the report. Write to corrections@inpalestine.today.
