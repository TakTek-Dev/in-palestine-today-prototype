    An editorial on the camps, before dawn

    00:23Sun

    Haaretz editorial: barring displaced Palestinians from going home would be population transfer

    Northern West Bank — The Israeli daily’s editors say a mass displacement is taking place in the West Bank in practice, with little public attention.

    The Israeli daily Haaretz said in an editorial published just after midnight that ‘an ongoing mass displacement of Palestinians from their homes is taking place in practice in the West Bank’, with little public attention.

    ‘Not giving them the opportunity to return would constitute population transfer, which is defined as a war crime,’ the editors wrote, warning that Israel could end up ‘in the dock’. The editorial ran hours after Israel’s High Court asked the state for a timetable for the return of more than 33,000 people displaced from the Jenin, Tulkarm and Nur Shams camps, and after Defense Minister Israel Katz called the army’s presence there ‘permanent’.

    Settlers posing as soldiers beat a Talfit family in their home overnight

    Talfit, south of Nablus — The father said masked men attacked him, his wife and their two daughters, aged 14 and 18, while the army was operating in the village.

    Settlers dressed as Israeli soldiers knocked on the door of a family home in Talfit, south of Nablus, before dawn on Tuesday and beat the family, injuring several of them, local sources told WAFA.

    The father told The Times of Israel that about five masked settlers attacked the household at around 3 a.m. while Israeli troops were operating in the village, beating him, his wife and their daughters aged 14 and 18. His wife needed 30 stitches to a head wound, one daughter had a broken leg and the other chest injuries, and all four were taken to hospital, he said. The Israeli military had not commented, the paper said.

    09:20Sun

    Dozens of settlers attack Turmus Ayya to keep residents from groves behind the wall

    Turmus Ayya, north of Ramallah — It was the second day running that Turmus Ayya farmers were kept from their olives.

    Dozens of settlers attacked the town of Turmus Ayya, north of Ramallah, on Tuesday morning to stop residents from reaching their land behind the separation wall to pick olives, local sources told WAFA.

    The Colonization and Wall Resistance Commission later listed Turmus Ayya among the day’s attacks, saying settlers stole olives and harvesting equipment, broke trees and drove owners off their land. WAFA cited the Commission as recording 647 settler attacks across the West Bank in September.

    10:35Sun

    Israeli forces surround Ramallah’s main hospital and raid rights groups; teen shot, man seriously hurt

    Ramallah — Two journalists were injured and dozens treated for tear gas during the hours-long raid, the Health Ministry and local sources said.

    Israeli forces entered the courtyard of the Palestine Medical Complex in Ramallah on Tuesday morning, posted snipers at its gates and stopped people from entering or leaving, local sources told WAFA. Troops also moved through al-Bireh, al-Tira and Surda.

    At the al-Tashri’i roundabout in central Ramallah, soldiers fired live and rubber-coated bullets and tear gas and targeted journalists, local sources said: a Palestine TV reporter was hit in the hand by a stun grenade and a Sky News Arabia reporter in the foot by fragments of a gas canister fired directly at him. Tear gas was also fired into the Ramallah boys’ school, WAFA said.

    A young man was seriously wounded by live fire and a 14-year-old boy was shot in the upper thigh, and dozens were treated for tear gas inhalation, the Palestinian Health Ministry said. Troops raided the offices of Addameer, the prisoners’ rights group Israel closed in 2022, and seized equipment and files, WAFA reported, and also raided the Union of Palestinian Women’s Committees, according to Quds News Network. Two men were detained after being beaten in the street, WAFA said.

    12:51Sun

    ‘Displacement is like you do not have a homeland’: Gazans tell the Red Cross about three years of war

    Gaza Strip — A father who stayed behind follows his son’s treatment abroad from a distance; a Red Crescent driver remembers the patients he took to the border.

    Three years after the escalation of the war, people in Gaza described to the International Committee of the Red Cross what they have lived through, in a feature published on Tuesday. A 34-year-old woman said she left home with only her ID card and a few clothes, expecting to return in two days; more than a year later she still lives in a tent.

    A 74-year-old man recalled the meals his late wife cooked to gather their children and grandchildren. ‘That was home,’ he said. A 38-year-old father said goodbye in 2025 to his wife and 11-year-old son, who was medically evacuated for treatment abroad, and has followed the boy’s care from Gaza ever since.

    13:15Sun

    Gaza death toll since October 2023 reaches 74,389, medical sources say

    Gaza Strip — Seven dead, three of them from earlier wounds, reached hospitals in 24 hours; 1,498 have been killed since the ceasefire.

    The number of Palestinians killed in Gaza since 7 October 2023 has risen to 74,389, with 175,423 injured, medical sources told WAFA on Tuesday.

    Hospitals received seven dead in the previous 24 hours, three of whom died of earlier wounds and one whose body was recovered, and seven injured, the sources said. Since the ceasefire of 11 October 2025, 1,498 people have been killed and 5,306 injured, and 838 bodies have been recovered, they said.

    13:22Sun

    Cabinet cites 719 attacks in one week and 38,000 women killed in three years of war

    Ramallah — The weekly session also backed a statement by 100 aid groups calling the Gaza ceasefire ‘ink on paper’ and set winter time from 24 October.

    Prime Minister Mohammad Mustafa told the weekly cabinet session on Tuesday that 719 attacks on Palestinians and their property were documented between 4 and 10 October, including burned vehicles, attacks on olive pickers, new outposts and the demolition of 27 structures, citing Colonization and Wall Resistance Commission data, WAFA reported.

    The Ministry of Women’s Affairs told the cabinet its records show 38,000 women killed in three years of war in Gaza, one in 21 women killed or disabled, and 22,000 women who lost their husbands, according to WAFA. The cabinet welcomed a statement by 100 international aid and rights groups that called the Gaza ceasefire ‘ink on paper’ and said about 1.4 million people face acute hunger.

    A call for protection

    16:38Sun

    Arab League calls for international protection for Palestinians and condemns Colombia’s embassy plan

    Arab League headquarters, Cairo — Meeting at Palestine’s request, the council urged the Security Council to act under Chapter VII and called for a ‘Uniting for Peace’ session of the General Assembly.

    The Arab League Council, meeting in an extraordinary session of permanent representatives in Cairo at Palestine’s request, called on the parties to the Fourth Geneva Convention to intervene immediately to protect Palestinians from Israeli attacks and settler violence, WAFA reported. Tunisia chaired the session.

    The council asked the UN Security Council to adopt a resolution under Chapter VII obliging Israel to comply with its resolutions, called for an emergency special session of the General Assembly under ‘Uniting for Peace’, and condemned settler attacks on farmers during the olive harvest, WAFA said.

    18:06Sun

    A year after the prisoner exchange, about 80 freed prisoners have been re-arrested or summoned

    West Bank and Israeli prisons — The 13 October 2025 exchange freed 1,968 Palestinians, 250 of them serving life sentences, the Prisoners’ Society said.

    Israeli authorities have re-arrested, summoned or interrogated about 80 Palestinians freed in previous prisoner exchanges, the Palestinian Prisoners’ Society said on Tuesday, the first anniversary of the 13 October 2025 exchange, according to Quds News Network.

    That exchange freed 1,968 Palestinian prisoners, 250 of them serving life sentences, and the three exchanges since November 2023 freed 3,985 in all, the society said. It said families of prisoners deported abroad on release are still unable to see them.

    ‘Our country, our control’, and the election delay

    19:36Sun

    Netanyahu, visiting near Tulkarm, says Israel will keep control of the area: ‘Our country, our control’

    Near Tulkarm — He warned Palestinian Authority forces against turning their weapons on Israeli troops before the election.

    Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu, visiting the area near Tulkarm with Defense Minister Israel Katz and senior security officials, said the army will keep control of the Tulkarm area, where it has carried out extensive operations, The Times of Israel reported.

    Netanyahu said Israel was ready for possible attacks in the West Bank, including by Palestinian Authority security forces turning their guns on Israeli troops, and vowed a response of ‘a level of force you have never imagined’. ‘There will be no Arab army west of the Jordan River,’ he said, according to the paper. The Times of Israel noted that attacks on Israelis by members of the PA forces are rare.

    22:32Sun

    Australia, Canada, France and Britain urge Abbas to reverse the election delay

    Ramallah — The four foreign ministers said a whole generation of Palestinians has reached adulthood without a vote, and asked Israel not to obstruct elections.

    The foreign ministers of Australia, Canada, France and the United Kingdom said they ‘deeply regret’ President Mahmoud Abbas’s decision to postpone legislative and presidential elections to September 2027, and called on the Palestinian Authority to reconsider and hold them ‘without further delay’, in a joint statement reported by Haaretz.

    They said nearly two decades have passed since the last legislative elections and that ‘an entire generation of Palestinians has reached adulthood’ without choosing its representatives. The ministers called on Israel to refrain from actions that could impede the elections, including the arrangements needed for all Palestinians to take part, and repeated that Hamas can have no role in governing Palestine.
